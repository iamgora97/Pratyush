Cardiovascular Health

❤️ Hypertension Awareness:
Know Your Numbers, Protect Your Health

Hypertension may be silent — but its complications don't have to be inevitable.

Hypertension Awareness • Preventive Health • Health Education
In this article
What is hypertension? Why it matters Symptoms Know your numbers Lifestyle Medicines Emergency The message

High blood pressure, or hypertension, is one of the most common chronic medical conditions worldwide. What makes it particularly dangerous is that it often causes no obvious symptoms, while gradually damaging vital organs.

That is why hypertension is often called the “silent killer.”

01

🩺 What is hypertension?

Blood pressure is the force exerted by circulating blood against the walls of our arteries.

When blood pressure remains consistently elevated, it is diagnosed as hypertension based on repeated measurements and the criteria used by the treating healthcare professional.

One high reading is not the whole story.

A single high reading does not necessarily mean that a person has hypertension. Blood pressure should be measured correctly and, when appropriate, confirmed with repeated home or ambulatory readings.

02

⚠️ Why is high blood pressure dangerous?

Untreated or poorly controlled hypertension can progressively damage blood vessels and increase the risk of:

❤️ Heart attack and heart failure
🧠 Stroke
🫘 Chronic kidney disease
👁️ Hypertensive eye disease
🩸 Peripheral arterial disease
Cognitive decline and other vascular complications

The longer blood pressure remains uncontrolled, the greater the cumulative risk.

03

🤔 Can hypertension have symptoms?

Usually, no.

Many people with significantly elevated blood pressure feel completely well. Headache, dizziness or fatigue can occur, but these symptoms are not reliable indicators of hypertension.

The only dependable way to know your blood pressure is to measure it.

04

📏 Know your numbers

Regular blood pressure monitoring is particularly important if you:

  • Have diabetes, kidney disease or cardiovascular disease
  • Have a family history of hypertension
  • Are overweight or physically inactive
  • Smoke or consume tobacco
  • Frequently consume high-salt foods
  • Are older
  • Have previously had elevated blood pressure readings
01 · Prepare Sit quietly for several minutes before measuring.
02 · Position Keep your arm supported at heart level.
03 · Equipment Use a validated upper-arm blood pressure monitor.
04 · Follow-up Take readings as advised by your healthcare professional.
Remember

Home readings can be useful, but interpretation and treatment decisions should be discussed with your healthcare professional.

05

🥗 Lifestyle: Your first line of defence

Healthy lifestyle habits can significantly improve blood pressure control.

🧂

Reduce excess salt

Limit highly salty and processed foods. Avoid adding unnecessary salt at the table.

🥦

Eat a balanced diet

Choose plenty of vegetables, fruits, whole grains, pulses and appropriate protein sources. Limit foods high in saturated fat, added sugars and excessive calories.

🏃

Stay physically active

Regular aerobic activity and resistance exercise, when medically appropriate, can help lower blood pressure and improve cardiovascular health.

⚖️

Maintain a healthy weight

Even modest weight reduction in people who are overweight can improve blood pressure.

🚭

Avoid tobacco

Smoking and tobacco use significantly increase cardiovascular risk.

🍺

Limit alcohol

Excessive alcohol consumption can raise blood pressure and contribute to cardiovascular risk.

😴

Prioritize sleep

Poor sleep can adversely affect cardiovascular health. Address persistent sleep problems with a healthcare professional.

🧘

Manage stress

Chronic stress can adversely affect cardiovascular health. Build healthy strategies for managing ongoing stress.

06

💊 Do I need medicines?

Some people can achieve adequate blood pressure control through lifestyle changes, while others require medication.

Medication is not failure

Antihypertensive medicines are an important tool for reducing the risk of stroke, heart attack, heart failure and kidney disease when indicated.

Take prescribed medication regularly and do not stop it suddenly or change the dose without medical advice, even when you feel completely well.

07

🚨 When is high blood pressure an emergency?

A very high blood pressure reading accompanied by concerning symptoms requires urgent medical assessment.

Seek emergency care

Seek emergency care if markedly elevated blood pressure is accompanied by symptoms such as:

Severe chest pain
Severe breathlessness
New weakness or numbness, particularly on one side
Difficulty speaking or understanding speech
Sudden severe headache
New confusion
Loss or significant disturbance of vision
Seizure or loss of consciousness

These symptoms may indicate a serious cardiovascular or neurological emergency.

08

❤️ The most important message

You don't have to feel sick to have hypertension.

Check your blood pressure regularly. Know your numbers. Make healthy lifestyle choices. If medication is prescribed, take it consistently and attend follow-up appointments.

Measure it.

Control it.

Protect your heart, brain and kidneys.

Hypertension may be silent — but its complications don't have to be inevitable.

This article is intended for general health education and does not replace individualized medical advice. Blood pressure targets and treatment decisions should be determined by a qualified healthcare professional based on your individual risk factors and medical conditions.
