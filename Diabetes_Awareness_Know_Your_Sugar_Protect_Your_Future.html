Diabetes Awareness:
Know Your Sugar, Protect Your Future

Detect early. Act early. Treat lows promptly. Protect your future.

Diabetes is a chronic condition that affects how the body regulates blood glucose (blood sugar). It is increasingly common worldwide and can affect people of all ages.

The challenge is that diabetes can develop silently. A person may have high blood sugar for months or even years before noticeable symptoms appear.

Early detection and good control can significantly reduce the risk of complications.

01

🩺 What is diabetes?

Glucose is an important source of energy for our body. Insulin, a hormone produced by the pancreas, helps glucose move from the bloodstream into cells.

In diabetes, the body either does not produce enough insulin, does not use insulin effectively, or both. As a result, blood glucose levels become persistently elevated.

The major types include:

Type 1 diabetes

An autoimmune condition in which the body destroys insulin-producing cells.

Type 2 diabetes

The most common form, often associated with insulin resistance and progressive loss of insulin production.

Gestational diabetes

Diabetes first diagnosed during pregnancy.

02

⚠️ Why is diabetes dangerous?

Uncontrolled diabetes can gradually damage blood vessels and nerves and increase the risk of:

❤️ Heart

Heart attack and cardiovascular disease

🧠 Brain

Stroke

👁️ Eyes

Diabetic retinopathy and vision loss

🫘 Kidneys

Chronic kidney disease

🦶 Feet

Foot ulcers and infections

Peripheral nerves

Nerve damage (neuropathy)

Many complications can be prevented or delayed through early diagnosis and good long-term glucose control.

03

🤔 What are the warning signs?

Some people have no symptoms at all.

Possible symptoms include:

  • Excessive thirst
  • Frequent urination
  • Increased hunger
  • Unexplained weight loss
  • Fatigue
  • Blurred vision
  • Slow-healing wounds
  • Recurrent infections

Don't wait for symptoms to appear before getting tested.

04

🔍 Who should consider screening?

Screening is particularly important for people with risk factors such as:

  • Overweight or obesity
  • Family history of diabetes
  • High blood pressure
  • Abnormal cholesterol or triglycerides
  • Previous gestational diabetes
  • Polycystic ovary syndrome
  • Physical inactivity
  • Previous prediabetes
  • Increasing age

Screening is an opportunity to act early.

Your healthcare professional can advise which test and screening interval are appropriate for you.

05

🥗 Diabetes prevention starts today

For many people at increased risk of type 2 diabetes, lifestyle changes can substantially reduce the risk of developing the disease.

🥦

Eat smart

Choose vegetables, whole grains, pulses, nuts and appropriate protein sources more often. Limit sugary drinks, excessive refined carbohydrates and highly processed foods.

🏃

Move regularly

Regular physical activity improves insulin sensitivity and supports a healthy weight.

⚖️

Maintain a healthy weight

If you are overweight, even modest, sustained weight loss can improve metabolic health.

🚭

Avoid tobacco

Smoking increases cardiovascular risk and adds to the health risks associated with diabetes.

😴

Sleep well

Adequate, regular sleep is an important part of overall metabolic health.

06

🚨 Hypoglycemia: Know What to Do

Hypoglycemia means blood glucose is too low. It is most commonly a concern in people using insulin or certain diabetes medicines.

Below 70 mg/dL

A glucose level below 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L) is commonly used as the threshold for hypoglycemia.

⚠️ Recognize the symptoms

Watch for:

  • Shaking or trembling
  • Sweating
  • Hunger
  • Fast heartbeat
  • Dizziness
  • Headache
  • Irritability or confusion
  • Difficulty concentrating
  • Weakness

Severe hypoglycemia can cause seizures, loss of consciousness or inability to treat oneself.

🍬 The 15–15 Rule

1
Take about 15 g of fast-acting carbohydrate.

If you are awake, able to swallow safely, and your blood glucose is below 70 mg/dL.

Glucose tablets / gel≈120 mL (4 oz) regular fruit juice≈120 mL regular non-diet soft drink
2
Wait 15 minutes.

Give the fast-acting carbohydrate time to work.

3
Recheck your blood glucose.

Check again after 15 minutes.

4
Repeat if necessary.

If it remains below 70 mg/dL, repeat the treatment.

Once your glucose has recovered, follow your usual meal/snack plan. If your next meal is not soon, a longer-acting carbohydrate-containing snack may be appropriate.

Important

Avoid chocolate or other high-fat foods as the first treatment because fat can slow glucose absorption.

07

🚑 Severe Hypoglycemia: Emergency Action

If the person is confused, having a seizure, unconscious, or unable to swallow safely:

Act immediately
Do NOT give food, drink or anything by mouth.
Place the person on their side if unconscious and breathing.
Give glucagon if it has been prescribed/available and someone is trained to use it.
Call emergency medical services immediately.
Stay with the person until help arrives.

After a severe hypoglycemic episode, medical review is important, particularly to identify the cause and prevent another episode.

📝 Prevent future episodes

  • Review insulin or medication doses with your healthcare professional.
  • Avoid skipping or delaying meals when this could cause low glucose.
  • Check glucose as recommended.
  • Carry a source of fast-acting carbohydrate.
  • Consider carrying a medical ID indicating that you have diabetes.
  • Make sure family members and close contacts know how to recognize and treat severe hypoglycemia.
Do not change treatment on your own

Never independently reduce or stop insulin or diabetes medication because of a low reading without medical advice.

08

📢 Don't Wait — Get Screened Today

You may feel perfectly healthy and still have diabetes or prediabetes.

🩸 Make today the day you know your numbers.

Visit a healthcare facility today and ask whether you should have your blood glucose checked.

A simple blood test can help identify diabetes or prediabetes and provide an opportunity to act early.

If you already have diabetes, use today as a reminder to review your treatment, glucose monitoring and follow-up plan.

Detect early. Act early. Treat lows promptly. Protect your heart, kidneys, eyes and nerves.
