🌬️ Asthma Awareness:
Breathe Better, Live Better

Asthma can be controlled. Asthma should not control your life.

Asthma Awareness • Preventive Health • Health Education

Asthma is more than just occasional breathlessness. It is a chronic respiratory condition in which the airways become inflamed and overly sensitive, causing episodes of coughing, wheezing, chest tightness and difficulty breathing.

The good news is that asthma can usually be effectively controlled with appropriate treatment, allowing most people to lead active and fulfilling lives.

01

What happens in asthma?

In asthma, the airways can become inflamed and narrowed. They may also produce excess mucus and become more sensitive to certain triggers.

Common symptoms include:

  • Cough, particularly at night or early morning
  • Wheezing or a whistling sound while breathing
  • Shortness of breath
  • Chest tightness
  • Symptoms that worsen with exercise, cold air, allergens or respiratory infections

Symptoms can vary from person to person and may come and go.

02

🌿 Common asthma triggers

Asthma triggers differ between individuals. Common triggers include:

Dust mitesPollenPet danderTobacco smokeAir pollutionStrong perfumes or chemicalsCold airExerciseViral respiratory infectionsWorkplace exposures

Identifying personal triggers can help reduce the frequency of symptoms.

03

💊 Inhalers are not addictive

Common misconception

Using an inhaler means asthma is severe or that a person will become dependent on it.

The message

This is not true. Inhaled medicines deliver medication directly to the airways and are an important part of asthma treatment.

Controller medicines, particularly those containing inhaled corticosteroids (ICS), help reduce airway inflammation and the risk of asthma attacks in people who need them.

Reliever medicines may provide rapid symptom relief, but frequent use of a reliever should prompt medical review.

04

🚨 Asthma Emergency Action Plan

An asthma attack can become serious quickly. Know what to do before an emergency happens.

🟢 Step 1 — Mild symptoms

If you notice increasing cough, wheezing, chest tightness or breathlessness:

  • Stop the activity and sit upright.
  • Stay calm and move away from any known trigger.
  • Use your prescribed reliever inhaler according to your personal asthma action plan.
  • Use a spacer if one has been prescribed or recommended.
  • Monitor your symptoms closely.
🟡 Step 2 — Symptoms are not improving

If symptoms continue, worsen, or keep returning despite your prescribed reliever:

  • Follow your written asthma action plan for additional doses and next steps.
  • Contact your doctor or seek urgent medical assessment.
  • Do not simply continue taking repeated doses without medical guidance.

If you have been prescribed an emergency course of oral corticosteroids, take it only according to the instructions given by your healthcare professional.

🔴 Step 3 — Severe asthma attack: GET EMERGENCY HELP

Call your local emergency medical service immediately if:

  • Breathing is severely difficult or rapidly worsening
  • You cannot speak in full sentences because of breathlessness
  • Your reliever is not providing adequate relief
  • You become very weak, drowsy, confused or exhausted
  • Your lips or face appear bluish or grey
  • You have a peak-flow reading in the red zone according to your personal asthma action plan
  • You feel that this is a life-threatening emergency
Emergency

Do not drive yourself to the hospital. While waiting for emergency help, remain upright and continue your prescribed emergency treatment according to your asthma action plan.

Important

Asthma treatment plans are individualized. The exact inhaler, dose and number of doses during an attack can differ between patients. Follow the written plan provided by your doctor rather than relying on a generic dosing schedule.

05

🩺 Preventing asthma emergencies

The best emergency plan is good asthma control.

Take your prescribed controller medication regularly.
Learn and regularly check your inhaler technique.
Know your personal asthma triggers.
Keep your reliever inhaler available as advised.
Have a written asthma action plan.
Attend regular medical reviews.
Discuss vaccinations and other preventive measures with your healthcare professional.
Make sure family members, caregivers and school/workplace contacts know what to do during an asthma emergency.
06

🏃 Asthma should not stop you from living

With good asthma control, many people can exercise, travel, work and participate in sports normally.

The goal isn't simply to treat symptoms when they occur. Good asthma management aims to maintain long-term control, reduce the risk of severe attacks and support a healthy, active life.

07

❤️ The message this Asthma Awareness period

Don't ignore recurrent breathlessness.
Don't fear prescribed inhalers.
Don't wait for severe symptoms before seeking help.

If you or your child frequently coughs at night, wheezes, becomes breathless during exercise, or repeatedly experiences breathing problems, consult a healthcare professional for proper assessment.

Breathe easier. Know your triggers. Know your action plan.

Asthma can be controlled.

Asthma should not control your life.
