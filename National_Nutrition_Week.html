National Nutrition Week

Nutrition Is Not Just About Food — It Is About Health
Nutrition · Preventive Health • Health Education
In this article
Healthy diet Carbohydrates Protein Sugar & salt Disease prevention Miracle diets The conversation

National Nutrition Week is an opportunity to remind ourselves of one of the most fundamental principles of medicine: good nutrition is the foundation of good health.

We often associate nutrition with weight loss, dieting, or appearance. But nutrition goes far beyond body weight. It influences immunity, muscle strength, brain function, hormonal health, cardiovascular risk, bone health, recovery from illness, and even the ability of the body to heal.

01 · THE BASICS

🥗 What does a healthy diet actually mean?

A healthy diet does not have to be complicated or expensive. The basic principle is balance, variety and moderation.

A balanced plate should ideally include:

  • Protein: pulses, beans, eggs, fish, chicken, dairy or other protein sources
  • Whole grains: rice, roti, oats, millets and other minimally processed grains
  • Vegetables: preferably a variety of colours
  • Fruits: whole fruits rather than juices
  • Healthy fats: nuts, seeds and appropriate amounts of plant oils
  • Adequate fluids: primarily water

No single food can provide everything our body needs. Dietary diversity matters.

02 · ENERGY

🍚 Carbohydrates are not the enemy

Carbohydrates are an important source of energy. The goal is not necessarily to eliminate them, but to choose better sources and appropriate portions.

Whole grains, vegetables, fruits and legumes generally provide carbohydrates along with fibre and micronutrients.

Similarly, fat is not automatically unhealthy. The type and quantity of fat matter.

03 · MUSCLE & REPAIR

💪 Protein deserves more attention

Protein is essential for maintaining muscle mass, producing enzymes and hormones, supporting immunity and repairing tissues.

Protein requirements become particularly important during childhood, pregnancy, ageing, recovery from surgery or infection, and in people at risk of muscle loss.

A common mistake is to focus only on calories while ignoring protein and overall nutritional quality.

04 · EVERYDAY CHOICES

🧂 Sugar, salt and ultra-processed foods

Excessive intake of added sugars and salt can contribute to long-term health problems.

Frequent consumption of highly processed foods can also make it easier to consume excess calories while getting relatively little fibre and micronutrients.

Reading food labels, controlling portions and choosing minimally processed foods more often can make a meaningful difference.

05 · PREVENTIVE HEALTH

🩺 Nutrition is part of disease prevention

Healthy nutrition plays an important role in reducing the risk of several chronic conditions, including:

Obesity
Type 2 diabetes
Hypertension
Cardiovascular disease
Fatty liver disease
Nutritional deficiencies
Individualized care matters

For people who already have a medical condition, however, dietary advice should be individualized. A diet appropriate for one person may not be appropriate for another—for example, nutritional requirements can differ significantly in kidney disease, liver disease, diabetes or pregnancy.

06 · BE SCEPTICAL

🚫 Beware of “miracle diets”

There is no universally perfect diet.

Detox drinks, extreme fasting, eliminating entire food groups without medical indication, or relying on supplements cannot substitute for a balanced dietary pattern.

Supplements should supplement a diet — not replace it.

07 · A BETTER CONVERSATION

🌱 This National Nutrition Week, let’s change the conversation

Instead of asking:

“How quickly can I lose weight?”

let's also ask:

“Am I getting enough protein, fibre, vitamins, minerals and healthy foods to keep my body functioning well?”

Good nutrition isn't a short-term diet.
It is a long-term investment in health.

«Eat for nourishment.
Move for health.
Sleep for recovery.
And make prevention a part of everyday life.»

Happy National Nutrition Week. 🥗❤️
